## 3. Act I — The Last Corrupted Foothold

### 3a. Recovery and departure

> *For the first time since you entered Brokenhill Mine, the air smells of cold stone instead of sickness. Tawny's new silver claws catch the morning light. Grumbold takes one enormous, sneeze-free breath. Beyond the trees, one patch of corruption still clings to the forest: the pond.*

**Direction:** Welcome them back with their victory, then let each player describe one recovery action. Resolve the safe Rest before another dangerous encounter. Do not replay the mine or retrospectively manufacture a reward scene. Filly's enthusiasm for future animal forms and Vega's elementals are level-4 hopes; they remain level 3.

If the players want to revisit old NPCs, let that happen briefly. Do not assume Marigold's mysterious whisper has been explained by this adventure. The “blue butterfly place” is **Enchanted Realm location 11**, not the later Wish Fish pool.

### 3b. Pranks and the pond riddle

🔗 **Run the keyed encounter from [[11_A_Pristine_Pond]].** Use the named spell/item rules below where old paraphrases differ.

> *Blue butterflies drift over a pond clear enough to show every pebble. Somewhere in the reeds, someone giggles. Then Filly's bootlaces pull together by themselves. Tawny looks from the knots to you with the expression of a creature who has wisely never worn shoes.*

**Direction:** One prank is enough if the table is eager; use all three if they enjoy it. Choose their actual clothing/gear, not an assumed tiara or a replacement hatchet.

- **Bootlaces:** WITS with advantage notices pixie traces. Failure gives a harmless stumble, not surprise damage.
- **Borrowed voices:** Vega sounds like Marigold, Marigold like Vega, Filly like Tawny. Shared laughter ends it; use the existing RESOLVE-with-advantage option if they resist by willpower.
- **Floating headwear:** GRACE with advantage to retrieve or WITS with advantage to spot the pixies. Let an inventive method work.

Joining the fun or showing Silver Rose Pins brings **[[Thistledown]]** into view. Her voice is delighted, then theatrically solemn: “Friends of the forest must still face our very difficult riddle.”

Read the riddle from the pond note or handout. Accept **moonlight, moonbeam, starlight, reflected light on water**. If needed, WITS with advantage or the hint “you can see it, but not hold it.” No stalled adventure because nobody guesses the intended wording.

**Reward:** one Dewdrop Elixir of Invisibility for the group; agree who carries it. Invisible for 10 minutes, ending on an attack or spell. It is not permanent invisibility or an automatic undetectable theft.

### 3c. The defense

> *The giggling stops. Something dark moves under the northern trees. Grass withers beneath dragging vines, and a tiny voice shouts, “They're coming again!” Thistledown draws a sword no bigger than a thorn. “Please. We can't lose the water.”*

**Direction:** Defend a place and its frightened inhabitants. Show the creatures' approach and give the PCs a moment to choose positions. The monster goal is reaching/corrupting the water; do not spend all attacks punishing the character who was already down last session.

| Monster | HP | SAVE | Armor | Attack | Special |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---|
| [[Vine_Crawler]] ×3, first wave | 4 | 8 | 0 | Vine whip d4 | **Pond variant:** a hit grapples; RESOLVE to break free. Vulnerable to fire/slashing in the existing encounter; no unlisted universal damage multiplier. |
| [[Vine_Crawler]] ×3, second wave | 4 | 8 | 0 | Vine whip d4 | Same; collapse when the Bloom is defeated. |
| [[Corruption_Bloom]] ×1, second wave | 6 | 6 | 0 | Toxic burst d6 | Fire deals double damage. On destruction, Nearby exposed PCs test RESOLVE or become Woozy. Purification avoids spores. |

**Pixie aid:** each PC has advantage on their **first attack each round** while near the pond. Do not stack it into extra dice or apply it to every defense and skill test.

**Encounter pace:** begin with three crawlers. Bring the second wave once those are dealt with or the party meaningfully stops them. Telegraph the Bloom as the controller: the vines twitch in time with its glowing center, and Thistledown points it out. Do not add creatures beyond the six crawlers and one Bloom to prolong a successful fight. If players solve the defense through terrain, purification, or another credible plan, honor that result.

**Water-edge hazard:** the location suggests GRACE with advantage when fighting on slippery banks. Use it for an actual risky maneuver near the edge, not a free additional check on every turn. Announce the risk to vulnerable supplies before a deliberate plunge.

⭐ **FILLY:** coordinate with Tawny, guard fleeing pixies, or strike the Bloom. She still has her spike; its actual sheet statistics apply. Reward her choice with the spear after the pond is saved, not only for personally landing the killing blow.

⭐ **VEGA:** use earth/stone gifts and Heartstone to block a vine approach, create a safe crossing, or expose the Bloom. Apply his actual Gift and Vein-Breaker rules; no automatic mass kill merely for owning the pick.

⭐ **MARIGOLD:** Healing Touch and the Staff support the endangered group. The established pond option is **RESOLVE with disadvantage** to purify the Bloom; success ends the attack without spores, failure uses her action. Radiant Wave uses [[Staff_of_the_Sunken_Sanctum]] as written; do not infer a new damage bonus from a descriptive line in old prep.

If the party retreats, keep the pond threat unresolved and reframe their next attempt from their plan. Do not announce the forest healed or award the spear while the outcome is still in doubt.

## 4. Act II — Amy Needs Help

### 4a. Aurelius's vision

🔗 [[Aurelius_the_Unicorn]] · [[Princess_Amy]]

> *Aurelius lowers his shining horn. “You have given this forest back its future. Now I must ask you to save someone else's.” In the gold mist above the grass, a young woman with a crooked crown shares a stolen saucer of cream with a cat. A squirrel objects from her shoulder. She laughs—and carefully pours him his own.*

**Direction:** Amy is **22**, as in the book. Use warmth and recognizable kindness, not an extended childhood biography. One or two images are enough: kindness to an overlooked servant; Scamp listening to a story. Then change the image.

> *The light cools. The squirrel is still there, but now it nestles beneath the chin of a black dragon. The dragon presses one claw against an invisible wall at the mouth of a cave. “I'll be brave,” she whispers. “Someone will come.”*

Aurelius names Amy and explains the **campaign tie**: the woman behind the corruption is **Queen Morwenna**, and Amy's Powerful Friendship together with his light may break the Barrier of Thorns. Amy must live. He remains to heal the land; the PCs' rescue matters.

| Question | Answer to give now |
|---|---|
| Where is Amy? | In the Scorchwoods, trapped in a dragon's form. Your pins can lead you to her. |
| Who is Morwenna? | The Queen behind the corruption, ruling from the White-Capped Mountains. This is the deliberate first-name reveal if it has not occurred. |
| Is she Lady Vespera? | No. Vespera was part of the Baron's story; Morwenna is the larger threat. |
| How do we reverse the change? | Amy can tell you what happened; the mechanism needs examination. No claim that the original key was destroyed. |
| Why not attack the fortress now? | Its thorn barrier resists the approach; rescuing Amy gives an ally and a way through. |
| Did Morwenna found the PPS? | Do not volunteer this. Aurelius declines the full old history: “There is much I should tell you. First, we must keep Amy alive.” Treat a direct, persistent investigation as a player choice; the default reveal remains the castle. |

**What stays hidden:** exact necklace solution, key holder, Ulrik's full role, Damon's command limits, and the PPS origin. Those are playable discoveries in the adventure, not a single briefing dump.

### 4b. Nightmare and dispatch

This is a **transition night's sleep**, before the Knight Tracker begins. If the party prefers immediate departure, frame the same omen as a brief waking vision from Aurelius rather than forcing an overnight stop.

> *In the dream, a black-armored knight walks toward the cave. A thorn-shaped mark stains his breastplate. Behind him, far away, a woman waits on a frozen throne. “Bring me her fire.” You wake to three matching Silver Rose Pins, warm against your skin.*

**Direction:** Omen, not a prediction that removes player agency. No damage, forced trauma, or tracker increase. The book's later orb vision remains its own event with its own roll.

**[[Nim_the_Badger]]** arrives with Daisy's dispatch: “Capital letters urgent. The kind where she does the scary voice.” Give **Handout A** from [[princess_and_dragon_handouts]]. Daisy provides names and a warning, not certainty about every knight or a guaranteed alliance. Preserve the PPS founder secret.

Aurelius blesses the existing pins as [[Silver_Rose_Fairy_Compass|Fairy Compasses]]: always point to Amy regardless of body; turn red in immediate danger. Explain that this fixed reference lets the party navigate where knights become lost. No repeated navigation rolls just to enter each keyed location.

## 5. Act III — Into the Dragon's Cave

### 5a. The hidden approach

🔗 [[The_Scorchwoods]] → [[01_Cave]]. Book printed pp. 6–9; detailed procedure in [[princess_and_dragon_runbook]].

> *The forest paths bend around you, but the pull of the pins stays steady. It leads beneath tangled roots, into a narrow passage and cold darkness. Ahead, a squirrel's alarmed chatter echoes through an opening. Something enormous whispers, “Scamp? Is that you?”*

**Direction:** Follow the book's cave opening. Do not send them to camp first merely because the old prep did. If they actively choose another destination before meeting Amy, use the runbook and start the hunt when exploration begins; retain the cave scene for their arrival.

### 5b. Dragon Panic

Amy turns, startled. Show the compass pointing to her, and run up to three panic rounds:

1. **Broken orb vision:** everyone tests RESOLVE. With three PCs, two failures mean initial Knight Tracker **3**, otherwise **2**.
2. **Falling stalactites:** GRACE or d4 damage.
3. **Accidental fire sneeze:** those in the path test GRACE or d6 damage.

After **each round**, one PC may test **GRACE with disadvantage** to calm her. Success stops remaining rounds; otherwise she calms after three. Do not make her deliberately attack friendly rescuers or use her full breath weapon instead of the panic hazard.

| NPC | HP | SAVE | Armor | Attack | Special |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---|
| [[Dragon_Amy]] | 24 | 16 | 3 | One bite d12 or claws d8, when relevant | Friendly and clumsy; normal physical evasion has advantage. The panic's hazards above are separate. |

⭐ **Marigold:** reassure Amy as a person, not a monster. The difficult calming roll belongs to the book; friends can support through the actual Heart Dice rules.

⭐ **Filly:** Scamp can recognize Amy and describe what he has seen. He is an animal companion, not an unlimited source of secret information.

⭐ **Vega:** inspect the necklace's workmanship. WITS or a relevant Talent recognizes gnomish origins. He has already seen how useful crafted magical tools can be.

### 5c. The problem and the choice

Let Amy tell the book's story; use runbook section 3. Do not recite all of it over player questions. Key facts: Embra used friendship to give her the necklace, used a key to lock the swap, sealed the cave, and ordered the hunt in Amy's body.

Give a choice grounded in what they learn:

- Find the false princess and her **original key**; Finnian is escorting her.
- Investigate gnomish craftsmanship and the nearby altar trail; Pippin can offer a second route.
- Seek help from or delay the hunting parties.

**Recommended session end:** Amy is calm, Scamp has reported the hunt, and the PCs choose their first lead. Fill [[princess_and_dragon_tracker]] with the starting value, Amy's HP, the party's destination, and who is staying with her. Do not roll the next session's events in advance.
