# Session 10 Scene 15: The Laughing Flowers

> **Session hub:** [[session10_prep]] · **Asset checklist:** [[session10_asset_checklist]]
> **Use when reached:** this is reserve material for the entire rescue. Move an unplayed note to a later session folder without changing its basename; update its session field/title then. Nothing here is established as played.
> **Run from this note:** narration, conversations, clues and scene rules are included. Other note links are optional background or continuation scenes.

**Foundry scene:** **Snapdragon Field** — refreshed backdrop.

## Arrival — a beautiful nuisance (10–15 minutes)

> *The trees open onto a sea of yellow flowers. Their long stems bow over a narrow path. The breeze moves across them in little shivers, and with each shiver comes a sound almost like someone trying not to laugh.*

**Direction:** Start gently. Let players notice the laughter before delivering the first joke; covering ears is a real choice. These flowers are a mischievous obstacle, not a combat encounter in disguise.

**A blossom near Filly:**

> “Oh, look. Visitors. Does the big cat know any jokes?”

**A second blossom:**

> “What is a dragon's favorite instrument? The horns!”

A third flower complains that everyone has heard that one. If the group smiles, keep going; if they are rushing, make the choice and test clear immediately.

## Crossing the field

Anyone who hears the flowers' jokes tests **RESOLVE**. Failure causes helpless laughter for an hour. Properly muffled ears give advantage. Describe the problem without choosing what a PC finds personally funny:

> *You try to take another step, but laughter catches in your chest again. The flowers bend together as though sharing an enormous secret.*

The illusion of a harmless joke is the danger: real time passes while Amy needs help. Losing time recovering someone or dragging them through raises the hunt **by 1 for this setback**, not once per laughing PC. No extra damage or ailment is specified. Other plans to carry, isolate the sound or bypass the patch should be judged on their actual merits.

**State the stakes:** “You can help them through, but dealing with this will cost time. What do you do?” Do not laugh for a real hour at the table.

## Beat them at their own game

Making the flowers laugh first prevents their volley. Accept a good player joke, a successful **WITS** test to invent a pun, or an appropriate performance Talent. Give the players a chance before requiring a test.

If the PC tells a wonderfully terrible joke:

> *One flower makes a tiny choking noise. Another repeats the punchline. Then the whole field folds in on itself, shaking with laughter, and the path opens between the bowed stems.*

**Flowers:** “Stop! Stop! Our sides! We haven't even got sides!”

Invite Marigold to soothe a laughing friend, Vega to deliver a deadpan stone joke, or Filly to translate Tawny's opinion. Those are invitations, not exclusive solutions.

## Spare jokes

Use just enough to establish the scene: “Dragons know their scales.” / “They prefer to wing it.” / “Their stories have long tales.” / “A knight and a slice of bread? With a little fire they're both toast.” / “No need to get fired up.” Never turn telling all the jokes into a required step.

## Exit

> *Behind you, the last yellow blossom calls, “Come back when you have a better audience!” The next tree creaks in a way that sounds suspiciously like a groan.*

Follow the route the players chose. The nearby Pool and Cottage are prepared separately; don't force the orchard or a knight encounter simply to fill time.

## Hunt procedure at this scene


Keep the recorded value across scenes. Start at **2**, or **3** if at least half failed the cave-orb RESOLVE test. No elapsed real-world time or session break advances it.

- Travel to a new keyed location: separate **d4 knight encounter** and **d4 Wrinkle** checks; each triggers on 1. A normal successful move adds no point by itself.
- **+1** for Spend Time, a Wrinkle, a wasted-time action/test, or letting encountered hunters leave unhindered. **+2** for an overnight Rest. **−1** for hindering or genuinely allying with a party; **−2** for defeating one. A living captain eating a gem apple gives −1, once willingly; Damon is unaffected.
- Table rulings: do not charge one wasted-time event twice; a separate Wrinkle adds its own point. Overnight +2 replaces ordinary Spend Time. Floor 0. Explain elective time costs first.
- **d6 Wrinkle:** 1: 2d4 hungry crystal wolves (HP4/SAVE6/Armor0; one or two attacks, bite d6/claws d4); 2: gnome calls for help; 3: knight party; 4: tear in a pack suggests a lost item, resolve fairly; 5: lost path; 6: poison-oak itching. Describe the problem and allow a response, rather than assigning an extra invented penalty.
- **d4 knights:** 1 Finnian, 2 Stella, 3 Raphael, 4 Damon/Ulrik. Use [[session10_14_knight_camp]] for their voices and profiles even when encountered on the trail; use the current location's backdrop.

**At 6:** choose among active hunters, excluding defeated, dissuaded or genuinely allied parties. If none remain, the hunt is neutralized. The pins turn red:

> *The silver rose against your chest warms sharply. Red light spills between its petals. The pull toward Amy has not changed, but now it feels terribly urgent.*

Finnian deliberates for one keyed move, then his party deals **4 damage per move/Spend Time** to Amy. Stella attacks immediately for **4**; diplomacy during her attack has disadvantage. Damon attacks immediately for **8**. Raphael causes no scheduled damage. Use Amy's actual remaining HP. Table interpretation: this is direct off-screen damage, without another attack roll or Armor subtraction. Once PCs intervene, play normally instead of adding off-screen damage on top. Do not automatically reset the tracker at 6 or force a particular hunter to arrive.
